## Section 1 — Findings that change the drafts

1. **No "health-service-provider necessity" exception in PDPL Art. 6.** Art. 6 lists exactly four no-consent cases (actual interest where contact is impossible; pursuant to another law or prior agreement; public-entity security/judicial processing; legitimate interest — unavailable for Sensitive/Health Data). None is a stand-alone "necessary for health-service provision" ground. The likely real basis is Art. 6(2) ("another law" or the treatment agreement), backstopped by IR Art. 26's cross-reference to MoH/Saudi Health Council/CHI rules. *Affected:* PRIVACY-NOTICE.en.md (line 60), ROPA.md (line 131), DPO.md (line 20). *Correction:* rewrite the "processed without consent because we are a health-service provider" sentence to cite Art. 6(2) + IR Art. 26, not an "Art. 6 necessity exception." *Source:* https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/PersonalDataProtectionLaw.pdf (Arts. 5, 6, 23). *Confidence:* MEDIUM (negative finding on Art. 6 itself is HIGH; the practical legal-basis conclusion is an inference).

2. **IR Art. 24 breach clock.** Notify SDAIA within **72 hours of becoming aware** (the trigger is awareness, not confirmation); phased/supplemented notification is allowed if information is incomplete, provided the delay is justified; notify affected data subjects **without undue delay** (no fixed period); Art. 24 does not override separate NCA duties. *Affected:* INCIDENT-RESPONSE.md (lines 82, 212, 216, 217), DPO.md (line 56). *Correction:* state the trigger as "awareness" explicitly and cite Art. 24(2) for phased notice. *Source:* https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/ImplementingRegulationPersonalDataProtectionLaw.pdf (Art. 24). *Confidence:* HIGH.

3. **Two non-identical breach-definition formulations.** PDPL Art. 20(1): "breach, damage, or illegal access." IR Art. 1(3): "Disclosure, Destruction, or unauthorized access." Neither uses "loss" or "alteration" the way the current GDPR-style draft definition does. *Affected:* INCIDENT-RESPONSE.md (line 15). *Correction:* reconcile the draft's definition with IR Art. 1(3)'s narrower wording, or have counsel confirm they are meant as synonymous. *Source:* both PDPL and IR URLs above. *Confidence:* HIGH.

4. **DPIA mandatory for sensitive-data processing.** IR Art. 25(1)(a) makes a written DPIA mandatory wherever Sensitive Data is processed (plus three other triggers: dataset linking; large-scale/new-tech/automated-decision processing of vulnerable groups; high privacy-harm risk). Prior consultation with SDAIA is **not** stated as a requirement. *Affected:* DPIA.md (lines 17, 338), DPO.md (line 54). *Source:* IR URL (Art. 25). *Confidence:* HIGH.

5. **DPO likely mandatory.** IR Art. 32(1)(c): DPO appointment is mandatory where the controller's core activities are based on processing Sensitive Personal Data. DPO Rules (SDAIA v1.0, Aug 2024) Art. 5 repeats and interprets this. *Affected:* DPO.md (lines 20, 22, 48/ROPA.md). *Source:* https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/RulesforAppointingPersonalDataProtectionOfficer.pdf (Arts. 2, 4-9); IR URL (Art. 32). *Confidence:* HIGH.

6. **ROPA retained 5 years after processing ends; required contents.** IR Art. 33(1): the processing-activities record must be kept for the entire processing period plus five years after that processing activity ends. Art. 33(5) sets expanded minimum contents (controller contact; DPO info; purposes; data/subject categories; retention periods per category; recipient categories; cross-border transfer description; security-measure description). *Affected:* ROPA.md (line 11). *Source:* IR URL (Art. 33); PDPL URL (Art. 31). *Confidence:* HIGH.

7. **National Register registration is mandatory, not optional.** The Rules Governing the National Register of Controllers, Art. 2: registration is mandatory for any of four independently sufficient triggers, including "the Controller processes Sensitive Data" — a hospital satisfies this trigger regardless of any other. *Affected:* DPO.md (line 60). *Source:* https://sdaia.gov.sa/Documents/TheRulesGoverningTheNationalRegisterOfControllersWithinTheKingdomPublicEN.pdf (Art. 2). *Confidence:* HIGH.

8. **IR Art. 26 health-data controls.** Beyond the general PDPL, IR Art. 26 requires minimum-necessary staff access, task/responsibility segregation, documentation of every processing stage, mandatory processor-agreement clauses for health-data work, and adoption of MoH/Saudi Health Council/CHI requirements. *Affected:* PRIVACY-NOTICE.en.md (line 60), ROPA.md (lines 115-117, 131). *Source:* IR URL (Art. 26). *Confidence:* HIGH (text) / MEDIUM (as applied to the hospital's specific legal-basis conclusion — see Finding 1).

9. **No published adequacy list; SCCs/BCR + Art. 7 risk assessment govern transfers.** As of the research date, no SDAIA "adequate countries" list could be found among SDAIA's current regulatory documents (absence-based). Absent that, a controller relies on an Art. 4 Appropriate Safeguard (SCCs, BCR, or a certificate) plus, where Art. 4 is relied on or sensitive data is transferred on a continuous/widespread basis, a mandatory Art. 7 Transfer Risk Assessment. *Affected:* DPA-AND-TRANSFERS.md (lines 47, 59, 167), ROPA.md (line 248). *Source:* https://sdaia.gov.sa/Documents/RegulationonPersonalDataEN.pdf (Arts. 3, 4, 7). *Confidence:* HIGH (Arts. 4/7 text) / MEDIUM (absence of an adequacy list — a negative finding).

10. **SDAIA guidance: remote access counts as transfer/disclosure.** SDAIA's Risk Assessment Guideline for Transferring Personal Data Outside the Kingdom (Feb 2025) states its methodology "includes remote access" within transfer/disclosure to entities outside the Kingdom — the closest primary-adjacent confirmation available, though the Guideline itself states it is non-binding. *Affected:* DPIA.md (line 115), PRIVACY-NOTICE.en.md (line 82), ROPA.md (line 59). *Source:* https://sdaia.gov.sa/en/SDAIA/about/Documents/RisksTransferringDataOutsideKingdomEn.pdf (p.13). *Confidence:* HIGH (guideline text) — note it is explicitly non-binding.

11. **Cloudflare Regional Services: Saudi Arabia is a named region (Enterprise-only); two other Data Localization Suite components are not available there.** The current Cloudflare region-support table lists "Saudi Arabia" individually (not under "Middle East") with Regional Services (TLS-termination confinement) available, but **Customer Metadata Boundary and Geo Key Manager unavailable**. The whole Data Localization Suite is an Enterprise-only paid add-on. *Affected:* DPA-AND-TRANSFERS.md (line 127), ROPA.md (lines 59, 115-117, 136), PRIVACY-NOTICE.en.md (line 78). *Source:* https://developers.cloudflare.com/data-localization/region-support/. *Confidence:* HIGH.

12. **Oracle's Saudi-specific transfer terms and support-staff access.** Oracle's "Data Transfer and Additional Jurisdiction Specific Terms Annex" contains a Kingdom of Saudi Arabia article (Art. 5, §I) subjecting transfers to non-adequate countries to Oracle's own "Kingdom of Saudi Arabia Standard Contractual Clauses (Controller to Processor)" in Exhibit G. Separately, Oracle's own blog states standard public commercial regions (which is what "Saudi Arabia Central (Riyadh)" is) are supported by Oracle's **global** 24/7 operations/support team, not staff confined to the region; no Saudi-specific residency-restricted personnel model (unlike EU Sovereign Cloud/Cloud for Government/Isolated Region) was found. *Affected:* DPA-AND-TRANSFERS.md (lines 104, 105). *Source:* https://www.oracle.com/contracts/docs/data-transfer-annex-v060225.pdf (via reader proxy); https://blogs.oracle.com/cloud-infrastructure/oracle-sovereign-cloud-operations-support-personnel-requirements. *Confidence:* MEDIUM (Annex, proxy-extracted, version-date discrepancy found) / HIGH (global-support-staff blog statement).

13. **OCI block volumes are encrypted at rest by default.** "The Block Volume service always encrypts all block volumes, boot volumes, and volume backups at rest... by default... using the Oracle-provided encryption keys" — no customer action needed; customer-managed keys are optional. *Affected:* ROPA.md (line 59, P1 row context). *Source:* https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Block/Concepts/blockvolumeencryption.htm. *Confidence:* HIGH.

14. **ECC/DCC/CCC scope is limited to government entities and Critical National Infrastructure (CNI); a private hospital is not automatically bound.** All three NCA instruments use an identical scope clause; other entities are only "strongly encouraged," not mandated, unless independently designated CNI, or unless a sector regulator (MoH/CBAHI/CHI-NPHIES) separately requires equivalent controls as a licensing/connection condition (suggested but not confirmed in a primary CHI/MoH document). *Affected:* DATA-CLASSIFICATION.md (line 16), DPA-AND-TRANSFERS.md (line 64), INCIDENT-RESPONSE.md (line 219). *Source:* https://cdn.nca.gov.sa/api/files/public/upload/86e09090-44e4-481f-bc28-355673607654_ECC--2024-EN.pdf; https://cdn.nca.gov.sa/api/public/cms/files/1c2cfb9c-7788-4549-9f29-f337500f1787_Data-Cybersecurity-Controls-.pdf; https://cdn.nca.gov.sa/ar/ccc-en.pdf. *Confidence:* HIGH (scope clause) / NOT FOUND (whether this specific hospital is CNI-designated — not publicly determinable).

15. **NDMO-derived classification likely places ordinary patient identifying data at Restricted, not Secret.** A public university's own instantiation of the national NDMO scheme lists ordinary identifying data (name, address, national ID, phone) as a **مقيّد (Restricted)** example, not Secret — "Secret" examples focus on life-threat/VIP harm, "Top Secret" on wide-scale health/safety impact. *Affected:* DATA-CLASSIFICATION.md (line 34). *Correction:* reconsider the draft's blanket "Secret" tier for all patient-identifiable health data against this generic national methodology; the info-security lead's own risk assessment should decide, not a rubber-stamp of either label. *Source:* https://www.psau.edu.sa/itdl/sitesuploads/itdl/2026-04/سياسة%20تصنيف%20البيانات_v1.4_13.pdf (§5-2). *Confidence:* MEDIUM (derivative source, not NDMO's own text; NDMO's own policy document could not be fetched this session).

16. **Medical-record retention instrument identified; the "10 years" figure is unverified.** The governing instrument is the Private Health Institutions Law's Executive Regulations (Ministerial Resolution No. 1019377, 28/5/1439H), Art. 3/3, which delegates the actual period to Ministry **Annex/Attachment No. 5** — not reproduced in the booklet obtained and not independently located. Several consistent secondary sources report "10 years from last visit, then destroy with a retained case summary; death/mortality records retained for the hospital's operating lifetime," but this is not a verified primary quote. *Affected:* DATA-RETENTION.md (lines 15-203, multiple), DPIA.md (lines 133, 273, 319), PRIVACY-NOTICE.en.md (lines 88, 116), ROPA.md (lines 29, 137, 154, 171, 204, 271, 277). *Source:* https://www.moh.gov.sa/Ministry/MediaCenter/Publications/Documents/222222.pdf (Art. 3/3); figure via e.g. https://www.studypool.com/documents/6131407/health-record-retention-in-saudi-arabia. *Confidence:* MEDIUM (governing article, primary) / MEDIUM (the 10-year figure and death-record exception, consistent secondary restatement, not a primary quote).

17. **Processor-contract minimum content.** IR Art. 17(1)(a)-(g): purpose; data categories; duration; processor's breach-notification duty; whether the processor is subject to another country's regulations; no-consent-needed-for-mandatory-disclosure clause; sub-processor identification. Art. 17(3): controller must periodically assess processor compliance. Art. 17(4): a processor that breaches instructions is itself treated as a controller. *Affected:* DPA-AND-TRANSFERS.md (line 47 context), ROPA.md (implicit processor rows). *Source:* IR URL (Art. 17). *Confidence:* HIGH.

18. **The Transfer Regulation is silent on in-Kingdom foreign-edge TLS termination.** No SDAIA guidance, FAQ, or regulation text was located that directly addresses a foreign provider decrypting traffic on hardware physically inside the Kingdom (Cloudflare's fact pattern absent Regional Services). A full-text search of the Transfer Regulation for transit/memory/encryption/server-location language returned zero matches. *Affected:* DPIA.md (line 115), ROPA.md (lines 59, 136), PRIVACY-NOTICE.en.md (line 82). *Correction:* flag as an open legal question for counsel's own analysis, not a research gap. *Source:* https://dgp.sdaia.gov.sa/wps/wcm/connect/e5bbede0-1119-4f70-b4ef-f043ce58d780/Regulation+on+Personal+Data+Transfer+Outside+the+Kingdom..pdf. *Confidence:* NOT FOUND.
